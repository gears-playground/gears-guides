Gears

A system for building software. It divides delivery into four gears, each with its own job, inputs and outputs. The gears are non-linear: engage whichever one the work demands, in whatever order it demands.

The four gears

  • Product
    how requests and feedback become scoped, prioritized product work.
  • Research
    how questions become evidence that informs design and development.
  • Design
    how product scope and research evidence become buildable specifications.
  • Development
    how specifications become shipped, reliable software.

How they connect

  1. Typed handoffsEach gear declares what it takes in and what it puts out, and one gear’s output is a legal input to another. That is what lets them run in any order rather than in a fixed line.
  2. IntakeEvery gear has an intake procedure — its own front door. Product’s is formalized as a sub-gear, Intake, the first of the three in its train.
  3. One gear at a timeBecause responsibilities are explicit rather than accidental, the system can be reasoned about, debugged and improved one gear at a time.
