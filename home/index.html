The Gears System

Gears

A system for building software. It divides delivery into four gears — each with its own job, inputs, and outputs. The gears are non-linear: engage whichever one the work demands, in whatever order it demands.

The four gears

  • Product — how requests and feedback become scoped, prioritized product work.
  • Research — how questions become evidence that informs design and development.
  • Design — how product scope and research evidence become buildable specifications.
  • Development — how specifications become shipped, reliable software.

Each gear is its own guide. Pick the one closest to the work you do.

Intake isn't a gear

Intake — capturing requests, feedback, and ideas — isn't a gear. It's a process that happens inside every gear, on that gear's own terms. Product's intake, for example, lives at product.gears.run/intake.

Why gears

Gears separates concerns that usually blur together. Each gear has its own inputs, outputs, owners, and instructions, so responsibilities are explicit instead of accidental. Because the gears are non-linear, you engage whichever one the work needs — without forcing everything through a single sequence. The result is a system you can reason about, debug, and improve one gear at a time.
