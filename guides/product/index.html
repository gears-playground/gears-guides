Product

Product decides what gets built, and checks whether it was worth building.

Takeaways

  1. OneProduct owns the question the other gears assume an answer to — whether to build the thing at all.
  2. TwoIt is the only gear that runs as a train. Three sub-gears turn in order, each completing before the next begins.
  3. ThreeObserve closes the loop. What shipped is measured against what was promised, and the result re-enters as new signal.

The train

  1. IntakeCaptures every request, screens what is worth pursuing, and routes it to the gear that can act.
  2. ManageCoordinates the work across gears, at whatever depth it needs — some items take a brief, others the full chain to a specification.
  3. ObserveMeasures what shipped against what the brief promised, and feeds the result back to Intake as new signal.

What comes out is a brief, user stories, a research request, a specification. Each one is a legal input to another gear, which is what lets the gears run in any order rather than in a fixed line.
